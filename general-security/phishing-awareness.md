# Phishing Awareness

Phishing is one of the most common and effective attack vectors used by cybercriminals. It exploits human trust and urgency rather than technical flaws. This guide covers what phishing is, how to recognize it, and how to defend against it.

---

## What is Phishing?

- **Phishing**: Fraudulent attempts to trick individuals into revealing sensitive information (passwords, credit card numbers, personal data) by pretending to be a trustworthy entity.
- Common delivery methods:
  - **Email phishing** (fake login pages, invoice scams).
  - **Spear phishing** (personalized attacks targeting individuals or roles).
  - **Smishing** (SMS text message phishing).
  - **Vishing** (voice phishing via phone calls).
  - **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** scams.

---

## Recognizing Phishing Attempts

- **Suspicious sender**: Check the sender’s email address closely for misspellings or unusual domains.
- **Urgency or fear tactics**: “Your account will be locked in 24 hours!” is designed to pressure quick action.
- **Unexpected attachments or links**: Malicious files or fake login portals.
- **Generic greetings**: “Dear user” instead of your actual name.
- **Mismatched URLs**: Hover over links — if the URL doesn’t match the supposed sender, it’s likely malicious.

---

## How to Defend Against Phishing

### For Individuals
- **Verify requests** before clicking links or opening attachments. When in doubt, contact the sender via a trusted method.
- **Check URLs** carefully before entering credentials.
- **Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** to reduce impact even if credentials are stolen.
- **Keep systems updated** (OS, browser, email client).
- **Report suspicious emails** to your organization or email provider.

### For Organizations
- **Email filtering & security gateways**: Block known malicious domains and attachments.
- **User training**: Regular awareness campaigns and phishing simulations.
- **Incident response process**: Define how employees should report phishing attempts.
- **Monitor for credential leaks** using threat intelligence and breach monitoring.

---

## Best Practices

- Think before you click: pause and inspect unexpected messages.
- Avoid entering credentials from an **email link** — go directly to the website.
- Be cautious of **QR code phishing (quishing)**, a growing attack trend.
- Encourage a **“report, don’t punish” culture** so employees feel comfortable flagging suspicious messages.
